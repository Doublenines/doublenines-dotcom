Computer Vision for Enterprise Document Analysis

April 9, 2025

Enterprise businesses run on documents — invoices, contracts, inspection reports, compliance forms, shipping manifests. The problem is that most of these documents are unstructured. They arrive as PDFs, scanned images, photos from the field, and handwritten notes. Extracting actionable data from them has traditionally been a manual, error-prone process.

We've been working with several enterprise clients to change that, using a combination of computer vision and multimodal AI to turn unstructured documents into structured, queryable data — automatically.

Why Traditional OCR Falls Short

Optical Character Recognition (OCR) has been around for decades, and it's gotten quite good at reading clean, typed text. But enterprise documents are rarely clean:

  • Scanned forms with inconsistent layouts across vendors
  • Handwritten notes and annotations on printed documents
  • Photos taken in poor lighting conditions on job sites
  • Multi-page documents where context spans across pages
  • Tables, charts, and diagrams embedded alongside text

Traditional OCR gives you characters. What enterprises need is understanding — knowing that this block of text is a line item amount, that signature means the document is approved, and this handwritten note is a critical exception.

Our Approach: Vision + Language

We use multimodal AI models that can see and reason about documents simultaneously. Instead of extracting text first and then trying to make sense of it, these models look at the entire document — layout, formatting, images, tables, and text together — and extract meaning directly.

The pipeline works in stages:

  1. Document classification — Automatically identify the document type (invoice, inspection report, contract amendment, etc.) regardless of format or vendor
  2. Layout analysis — Understand the spatial structure of the document, identifying headers, tables, signatures, stamps, and free-form text regions
  3. Field extraction — Pull specific data points based on the document type — line item amounts from invoices, deficiency codes from inspection reports, clause modifications from contracts
  4. Validation and confidence scoring — Each extracted field gets a confidence score, with low-confidence extractions flagged for human review

Real-World Applications

We've deployed this approach across several industries:

  • Fleet management — Automatically processing vehicle inspection reports, maintenance records, and compliance documentation
  • Beverage industry — Extracting product and pricing data from distributor invoices across dozens of different formats
  • Financial services — Parsing complex multi-page contracts to identify key terms, obligations, and risk factors

The Human-in-the-Loop Pattern

No AI system achieves 100% accuracy on complex documents, and in enterprise contexts, errors have real consequences. That's why every system we build includes a human review workflow.

The key insight is that AI doesn't need to be perfect to be valuable — it needs to be good enough that human review is fast. When the system extracts 95% of fields correctly and highlights the 5% it's uncertain about, reviewers can process documents 10x faster than doing everything manually.
