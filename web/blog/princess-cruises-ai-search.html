How We Built AI-Powered Search for Princess Cruises

October 27, 2025

When Princess Cruises approached us, they had a classic search problem: thousands of itineraries across dozens of destinations, and customers who didn't know exactly what they were looking for. Traditional keyword search wasn't cutting it — travelers don't search for "7-night Western Caribbean sailing departing Port Canaveral." They search for "warm beach trip in January that's good for families."

The Challenge

Princess Cruises' catalog includes thousands of sailings with complex attributes — itinerary, ship class, ports of call, onboard amenities, pricing tiers, seasonal availability, and more. The existing search experience relied on rigid filters that forced users to already know what they wanted before they could find it.

Our goal: build a natural language search experience that understands intent, not just keywords.

Our Approach

We started by mapping the most common ways travelers describe their ideal cruise — not in database terms, but in human terms. Things like:

  • "Something romantic for our anniversary in the Mediterranean"
  • "A family cruise with lots of kids activities under $3,000"
  • "Alaska cruise with glacier viewing, departing from Seattle"
  • "Short weekend getaway from Miami"

From there, we built a pipeline that combines semantic search with structured filtering. The natural language query gets processed by an LLM that extracts intent, preferences, and constraints. Those get mapped to structured attributes in the catalog, and results are ranked by semantic relevance — not just keyword match.

Technical Architecture

The system has three core components:

  • Query understanding layer — An LLM that parses natural language into structured intent (destination preferences, travel dates, budget range, party composition, activity interests)
  • Hybrid retrieval engine — Combines vector similarity search on cruise descriptions with traditional database filtering on hard constraints (dates, pricing, availability)
  • Ranking and personalization — Results are re-ranked based on relevance confidence, with explanations for why each result matches the query

Results

The AI-powered search experience launched to positive reception from both Princess Cruises' internal teams and their customers. Early metrics showed meaningful improvements in search-to-booking conversion rates and a significant reduction in customer service inquiries about finding specific itineraries.

More importantly, the system handles the ambiguous, conversational queries that traditional search simply can't — which is exactly how most travelers actually think about planning a cruise.

Lessons Learned

Building AI search for a real enterprise product taught us several things we now apply across our engagements:

  1. Start with user language, not data structure. The most important step was studying how real travelers describe their ideal trip — then working backward to the data model.
  2. Hybrid beats pure AI. Pure semantic search produces impressive demos but unreliable production results. Combining AI understanding with structured filtering gives you the best of both worlds.
  3. Explainability matters. Users trust search results more when they can see why each result was recommended. "This matches because you mentioned family activities and Caribbean" builds confidence.
